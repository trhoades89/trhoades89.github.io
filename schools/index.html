      About the data & caveats

      About the data

      Everything here comes from official open data published by the Department for Education and Ofsted, rebuilt automatically each week. The sources are:

        Ofsted ratings

        Ofsted stopped giving single-word overall judgements in September 2024, and since November 2025 inspections produce report cards graded across several areas. The dot colour uses the most recent overall effectiveness judgement a school has received, which may be several years old. The school panel shows the date, the newer sub-judgements and any report-card grades, so check those before relying on the colour.

        Catchment areas

        England has no national catchment dataset. Most oversubscribed schools admit on distance, and the effective catchment moves each year with demand. The “approximate catchment” shown is the area that is closer to this school than to any other state school of the same phase (a Voronoi cell). It is a rough guide only. Faith, selective and independent schools usually don’t admit primarily on distance. Always check the local authority’s admissions booklet, which lists the last distance offered.

        Admissions

        Demand figures come from DfE “Primary and secondary school applications and offers”. They show how many families put the school as first preference compared with the number of offers made at the normal point of entry (Reception or Year 7).

        Performance

        Exam results come from the DfE school performance tables (“Compare school and college performance”). Small cohorts are suppressed by DfE. There are no KS2 or KS4 progress scores for recent years, because the pandemic cancelled the baseline assessments they depend on.
